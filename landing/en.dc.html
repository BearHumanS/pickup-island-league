ABOUT

A season that never ends

Pickup Island League is a fictional pro league played on courts all over the island. Every player is a 3D pixel model on the floor, and nothing is scripted: the game engine decides every possession as it happens.

Two AI commentators call it live, big plays come back in slow-motion replays and highlight Shorts. When a season ends there is an awards night, retirements and the Hall of Fame, the draft and free agency, and then the next season tips off.

PATCH NOTES

Patch notes

v107

LATESTOct 30, 2026 · A defense that stays with its man
  • Counting "abandoned" men, then fixing it: We measured half-court moments where an attacker inside shooting range was left with no defender within 3 m for over a second: 4.2 a minute. In 76% the defender had chosen that spot on purpose, half the time he stood above his man, and 69% of the time he was ball-watching with his man behind his shoulder.
  • Three causes: The defensive field's scoring ① credited closeouts far too generously (2.3 m covered for free), so sagging cost nothing, ② allowed nearly 3 m of cushion even one pass away, and ③ had no penalty for standing above your man or for losing sight of him.
  • The fix: Closeout credit cut to a step and a half (1.4 m max), a 2.1 m leash one pass away, help only below your man, and a preference for spots that keep both ball and man in view. Result: 4.2 → 2.3 a minute. What remains is mostly help rotations on drives (the far man is open in real basketball too) and zone defense.
  • Calibration: 72-game sample: eFG 54.3%, three-point rate 43.0% (down from 44.0 in v106), 35.9% from three, 0.27 free-throw attempts per shot, 13.8 turnovers and 19.6 fouls per 100 possessions, 12.6% of points in transition.

v106

Oct 30, 2026 · A zone that reads, a transition defense that talks
  • The zone reads its areas: Zone defense is no longer "a fixed spot plus 22% toward the ball". Every defender owns an area and picks where to stand by reading who is in it, whether the ball can get there and how dangerous he is. A cutter crossing areas is handed to the next man (the bump), a flash to the high post is taken by the nearest free defender, and an overload on one side pulls the weak side to the middle. The back line keeps the paint first.
  • Transition defense talks: For the first five seconds before the defense is set, the men running back sort the matchups out loud: rim first, ball second, shooters to whoever can get there. Bigs take the rim over a fast-break handler, and the man who is dangerous now over the man they usually guard. Low defensive IQ or poor chemistry means sometimes nobody calls it, two men take one and a shooter is left open.
  • Then the scram: Once the half court is set, the existing scram switch quietly restores the natural matchups. The transition talk and the half-court scram now connect, so the old scene of leaving the ball handler to chase a "mismatch" is gone.
  • Calibration: 72-game sample: eFG 55.2%, 35.9% from three, 0.26 free-throw attempts per shot, 13.8 turnovers and 19.3 fouls per 100 possessions, 13% of points in transition. In a four-game experiment pitting a zone team against a man-to-man team, the zone gave up fewer points (300 to 312).

v105

Oct 29, 2026 · Players who read the screen
  • Reading the set play: The man coming off a pin-down, flare or stagger no longer runs the line as drawn. If his defender chases over the top he curls inside; goes under, he pops for the shot; top-locks him, he goes backdoor; sits high, he fades to the corner. If the chase changes mid-route, so does the exit. Reading the screen takes off-ball IQ; a poor reader just runs the play as drawn.
  • The screener reads too: After the screen, if his own man went with the cutter he slips to the rim; a shooter pops to the weak side; otherwise he ducks in. Pick-and-roll roll-or-pop now also reads the big's defender: dropped deep, pop into the space; came up, roll hard behind him; switched onto a small, roll and seal.
  • The hammer exception: In the hammer set, if the defender beats the shooter to the corner, he lifts to the wing instead.
  • Goals pull, defenders talk: A set play now hands a player a goal spot; the actual route is re-evaluated by the off-ball field every 0.3–0.5 seconds. When the goal is sealed off he abandons it for open space, so the same set plays out differently every time.

v104

Oct 29, 2026 · Bodies, coaches' minds, players' hearts
  • Bodies collide: Every player now has a mass and a body radius, and bumps carry momentum: the lighter man gives ground and loses speed, the stronger man goes through the chest, and box-outs and post-ups hold by mass × strength × stance. A much bigger man at full speed can, rarely, put a small one on the floor.
  • Fouls come from contact: Charges, blocks, holds, push-offs, hand-checks, moving screens and over-the-back calls are no longer probability tables; the whistle reads how hard the contact was, who initiated it and whether the defender was set. Shooting fouls look at whether bodies just met.
  • Coaches with personalities: Each coach has three traits: adaptability, gambling and trust in his starters. A quick coach calls timeout on a 7-0 run, a stubborn one rides it out; a gambler hunts the man in foul trouble; a trusting coach keeps a tight rotation.
  • Scouting reports: Game plans read the season numbers. Sag off a team that can't shoot and take away the drive, jump the lanes against a turnover-prone team, get back before crashing the glass against a running team, hedge high on the pick-and-roll against a handler with a pull-up three.
  • In-game adjustments: Coaches keep score on each pick-and-roll coverage and swap the one that bleeds, and re-plan at halftime with the first half in front of them. A hot hand stays on the floor even when tired; in a 20-point garbage-time game the starters sit.
  • Tempers in the game: Misses, turnovers, fouls and "I was open and he didn't look" move a player's mood. Proud and hot-headed players swing hard — forcing shots, stopping the ball, fouling harder, picking up technicals — while calm and quiet players barely move. A leader or a cheerful teammate on the floor settles them faster, and the commentary reads it off their faces.
  • Duos and the locker room: Pairs that keep connecting on assists look for each other more and add to chemistry. Between games the locker room produces stories — minutes complaints that a coach or captain talks down or that sour the room, arguments during a losing streak, a captain calling a players-only meeting, a winning streak lifting everyone — in the news and the pre-game storylines. Cards now show morale, contract outlook and a player's partner.
  • Calibration: The first pass with contact physics had a bug that drained energy whenever bodies touched, which slowed the whole game; fixed, then re-calibrated over 72 games to 0.26 free-throw attempts per shot, 19.2 fouls per 100 possessions and 13% of points in transition. The mix of charges, blocks and holds now sits near real proportions too.

v103

Oct 29, 2026 · Drive paths and self-play
  • Drives built from pieces: a drive is no longer "straight at the rim". The handler picks from nine paths — three widths by three depths — by predicting where the defenders will be half a second later and taking the emptiest lane. Good finishers go deeper; low passing-IQ players sometimes pick the wrong lane.
  • Retreat dribble: under pressure a player can back it out, drag the defense with him and reset, more often when two defenders are on him.
  • Self-play learning pipeline: eighteen decision weights can now differ per team, and an evolutionary search has candidate settings play hundreds of games against the current ones, looking for settings that win more while staying inside real box-score ranges. The first run's champion gained only +0.3 points per game (±2.1) over 48 verification games, so no defaults changed this time. The pipeline runs again after every major change.
  • Rating inflation damped: a six-season simulation showed average OVR creeping up, so young-player growth was trimmed slightly.
  • Box-score balance: free-throw rate up, fast-break share down a touch, checked on a 72-game sample.

v102

Oct 29, 2026 · Players who read each other
  • Shared intentions: players announce their plan — a cut, a spot-up, a flash — and teammates use it: fill the spot a cutter just left (his defender went with him), stay out of where he is heading. When a pick-and-roll runs, the weak side lifts and the strong-side corner stays home.
  • Two passes ahead: good passers (pass vision and passing IQ) weigh what the receiver can do next. More extra passes, skip passes to the far side, and passes to a teammate who can attack the closeout.
  • Memory across the season: players remember what worked against each opponent. If the pick-and-roll hurt a team last time, they go back to it in the rematch, and the commentators note it. Kept small: the last four opponents per player.
  • Screen side and break lanes: which side a pick-and-roll screen goes to (open space, preferred hand, where the defender shades) and which lane a runner takes on the break (away from the retreating defense) are now read, not rolled.
  • Commentary and highlights for the new reads: blind-spot cuts, denial, filling the vacated spot, wing lifts, tight stances on shooters, box-out wins, jump passes and extra passes get called. Backdoor scores, double clutches and jump-pass assists rank higher in highlights and Shorts.
  • Performance: game logic costs 23% more than v98, still about 1.2% of real time, so the broadcast is unaffected.

v101

Oct 29, 2026 · On-ball defense and rebounding
  • On-ball stance by the numbers: the man guarding the ball picks from five distances and five shades the stance that leaves the ball handler's best option — shot, drive to his strong hand, drive to his weak hand, or pass — weakest. Shooters get crowded, slashers get cushion, and the favorite hand gets taken away. Pressure tendencies, foul trouble and where the help is all count.
  • Box-outs by the numbers: when a shot goes up, each defender chooses whether to box out, go get the ball or leak out, weighing how dangerous his man is on the glass, how close to the rim he is and who wins the bump.
  • Who crashes: the offense sends only players whose shot at the board is worth more than getting back, and someone always gets back.
  • Memory within a game: options that have worked tonight — the pick-and-roll, the drive, the post-up — get used more, and what has been shut down gets used less. High shot-IQ players notice sooner.
  • Box-score balance: threes, mid-range, fouls and blocks re-tuned on a 72-game sample.

v100

Oct 29, 2026 · Defense that thinks for itself
  • Positioning by points allowed: off-ball defenders no longer follow a fixed formula. They weigh about 20 places to stand by how many points each would give up: how open their man would be on the catch, whether he could go backdoor, and whether they could get in the way of a drive.
  • Deny or help, depending on the moment: a shooter one pass away gets his passing lane taken away, while a man far from the ball gets left for help in the paint. Overplaying the lane invites the backdoor, and the defense knows it. Help tendencies and IQ make each defender read it differently.
  • Reading the floor in transition: players trailing the break find good spots and spread the floor as soon as they cross half court.
  • An offense that waits: once a player reaches a good spot he holds it, ready for the catch, and moves again when the ball changes hands or help arrives. Less constant running around.
  • Balance: slightly fewer pass interceptions and holding fouls on cutters.

v99

Oct 29, 2026 · Off-ball instincts
  • Reading the floor every moment: instead of picking from a few fixed spots, off-ball players weigh about 30 places around them every third of a second: what a catch there would be worth, whether the pass can get there, spacing with teammates, and keeping the driving lane clear.
  • One beat ahead: when a teammate drives, players work out where the help will come from and slide to the spot it leaves open. Corner drifts, 45-degree lifts and filling the empty spot come out of the reading, not out of scripted rules.
  • Defenders have eyes: a defender watching the ball tracks a man behind his shoulder a beat late, less so with high help-defense IQ. Backdoor cuts into that blind spot happen on their own.
  • Every player sees it differently: players with high off-ball movement and shot IQ find the right spot; others sometimes stand in the wrong place. Cutting and spot-up tendencies and off-ball roles shape the choice.
  • Natural movement: players stick with a plan instead of changing it every second, and run in arcs off the ball rather than right angles, planting hard only on a real cut.
  • Assist rule: an assist now needs the basket within 4 seconds of the pass, with the scorer using no more than one dribble move.

v98

Oct 29, 2026 · Players who think in expected points
  • A new decision engine: instead of a fixed list of rules, the ball handler scores every option the same way — shoot, drive, a pass to each teammate, pick-and-roll, post-up, a dribble move, a fake or swinging it — by how many points it is likely to produce. Ratings set those values and tendencies tilt them.
  • Basketball IQ is judgment: players with high passing and shot IQ keep picking the best option, while low-IQ players sometimes make the wrong read. Fatigue blurs judgment a little.
  • Decisions that change with the clock: early in the shot clock players look for a better chance; as it runs down they settle for lesser shots. Passes weigh the risk of a steal and whether the receiver can shoot right away.
  • Off the ball: players move to where a catch would turn into the best shot, and keep the driving lane clear for teammates.
  • Defense: weak shooters get sagged off and shooters get crowded. Help comes off the least dangerous man, harder against good finishers, and rotations leave the least dangerous player too.
  • Auto-tuning: the engine's settings were tuned over hundreds of simulated games to match real pro box scores.
  • Chat commands in English: !card, !teaminfo, !join, !draft, !me, and fan play votes !tempo · !three · !inside · !press. Old commands still work.

v97

Oct 28, 2026 · Defenses that learn during the game
  • Reading drives: when a player keeps driving to the same side, his defender starts sitting on it. Defenders with high help-defense IQ pick it up faster, and the driver goes the other way more often.
  • Guarding the hot hand: a shooter who is on fire gets guarded tight, and nobody helps off him. The commentators call out the adjustment.
  • Fatigue changes choices: tired players look to pass before driving and cut, leak out and crash the glass less. On defense they give more room and help later.
  • Defenders play their own way: help-minded defenders sag deeper into the lane, and gamblers sit in the passing lane one pass away.
  • Back-down vs face-up: in the post, power players back their man down while finesse players turn and face, depending on their tendency.

v96

Oct 28, 2026 · Decisions in mid-air
  • Jump passes: a player who goes up for a shot and sees a shot blocker coming dishes it to the open man in mid-air, including dump-offs to the big under the rim. Pass-first players with high passing IQ do it more often.
  • Double clutch: when a defender reaches in on a layup, the finisher brings the ball down and goes back up as the defender lands.
  • Mid-air adjustment: with a hand in his face on a jumper, a shooter hangs a beat and shoots around it.
  • Pass fakes: from the triple-threat position, a fake pass pulls the help over, then he passes the other way, drives or shoots.
  • Pivots: after picking up his dribble under pressure, a player pivots away to open a passing angle.
  • Fakes into the next move: if a help defender leaves his feet on a pump fake, the ball goes to the man he left; after a jump stop into a crowd, the ball goes out to the open teammate.
  • Instant reads: when a defender stumbles, a cutter comes open or a help defender leaves his man, the ball handler reacts right away instead of waiting for his next decision.
  • Rebounds: an offensive rebound in traffic gets kicked out to a shooter, and a small defender can swipe the ball away as a rebounder lands.

v95

Oct 28, 2026 · Fast breaks and turnovers
  • More fast breaks: teams run off defensive rebounds more often, and players who leaked out become the first runners. About 12% of points now come before the defense is set, in line with real pro games.
  • Fewer moving-screen calls: with more screens being set, illegal screens were called far too often. They are down by more than half, and good screeners get called less.
  • Fewer three-second calls: players in the dunker spot now wait just outside the lane.
  • Box-score balance: re-measured over 54 games. Turnovers, steals, fouls and shooting efficiency sit closer to real pro numbers.
  • Language picker: the rulebook name is gone from the language descriptions.

v94

Oct 28, 2026 · Every player plays his own game
  • 36 tendencies: every player now has 36 habits across shot selection, creating, passing, off-ball play and defense, and the AI reads them on every decision. A high three-point taste means more threes; a lane gambler jumps passes and sometimes gets burned.
  • Two new ratings: off-ball movement and screening. Solid screens hold defenders longer; good movers find open space. Release speed and shooting hand now show on the card.
  • Nine off-ball roles: corner spacer, spot-up shooter, movement shooter, cutter, dunker spot, post anchor, high-post hub, pop big and transition runner. Where a player stands without the ball depends on his role.
  • 19 player types: labels like floor general, 3&D and rim protector appear on the card and the league site, and the commentators bring them up.
  • Pick-and-roll: bigs who can shoot pop to the arc instead of rolling, and handlers sometimes reject the screen and go the other way.
  • Six new signature moves: hesitation, between-the-legs combo, half hook, trailer three, slap steal and outlet pass. Some players learn a new move over the summer.
  • Habits that change: after a season of made threes a player shoots more of them, and after a cold season fewer. Assists and field-goal percentage carry over the same way.
  • Transition defense fix: defenders no longer leave the ball handler open while hunting for their usual matchup. The closest defender between the ball and the rim picks him up first.
  • Three-sided cards: click a player to flip between the front, the full ratings and the tendencies. !card in chat shows the tendencies too.

v93

Oct 28, 2026 · Defense and off-ball movement
  • Knocked-down bug fixed: A player who fell once stayed "on the floor" for the rest of the game as far as the AI was concerned, so he never helped on defense, got back in transition or chased loose balls. Fixed.
  • Transition defense: The first man back protects the rim and the next one stops the ball. No more defenders running past the ball handler to pick up someone else.
  • Paint defense: The closer a man is to the rim, the tighter he is guarded, and cutters get chased all the way. Unguarded players near the basket are far rarer.
  • Zone defense: Zone used to cover over a third of half-court possessions. It is now an occasional change-up, as in real pro basketball.
  • Pass types: Bounce passes are for short range. A long pass with a hand in the lane goes over the top.
  • Off-ball movement: Players cut into an empty paint, flash into gaps at the elbows, high post and short corners, and come to meet a handler who is stuck. They also reposition more often.

v91

Oct 28, 2026 · Coaches matter
  • Coach ratings: Every coach now has offense, defense, development and leadership ratings. Offense and defense nudge shooting percentages, development speeds up players 24 and under, and leadership softens morale drops after losses. Small effects, like in real pro basketball, that add up over a season.
  • Coaching styles: Up-tempo, defense-first, three-point and motion coaches lean the automatic game plan their way.
  • Coaching careers: Career record, titles and previous jobs are tracked. Fired coaches wait in the coaching market and can be hired again by another club.
  • Prediction points: Call a game right before tip-off for +10, or +2 just for playing. Naming a rookie with !draft costs 30 points, up to two per season. Members and Super Chats skip the cost. Check your balance with !me.
